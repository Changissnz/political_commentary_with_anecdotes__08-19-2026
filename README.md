It is about 9 PM, where I am at. This is in Charlotte County, FL. A little anomaly about half 
an hour ago when I was biking to the convenience store. Just as I was passing a Planet 
Fitness, there were two Asian females crossing the road to hop into a truck. One of them 
said to the other "[BLAH BLAH BLAH] Chut Mae [BLAH BLAH BLAH]" before they entered the 
vehicle. "Chut Mae" is Vietnamese for "mother dies", which translates to something like 
"mother fucker" (accusatory) or "God damn it" (exclamatory) in the English language. 

**I can understand Unterlang.** You know how in biology, there are certain traits in an 
organism that become vestigial (remnant, relic) through evolution? That's how I sort of 
feel about being able to understand "chut mae" from that Chinese doll. Because of the 
commercial dominance of China over in the East, my mind frequently assigns Vietnamese 
under the Chinese umbrella. Their national flags say something: both are red and yellow, 
the bigger one has five stars and the smaller one has one star. 

**I know, I know. It is a somewhat flawed Roman approach to understanding the people.** 
I did not start out life thinking that human language and culture had these scientific 
divisions, Unterlang and Uberlang. Conditions encouraged me to guard my originally open 
mind. Here's how I think about human affairs now. If I start off with the premise that 
some people, maybe influential people, do not have my best interests at heart, I will 
entertain less options but achieve the things that lofty dreamers miserably fail at for 
attempting to keep jumping higher and higher, thinking one success can easily lead to 
the next like a snowball effect. Truthfully, I feel this approach is only fair after I 
consider how some subsets of Greek and Judeo cultures handle things. One keeps it in 
the bloodline and the other in the faith. With me, my approach is more like an 
empiricist with half-formed hypotheses because to be naive like every thing is a new 
beginning is to inevitably be led, sometimes to Hell. 

**John F Kennedy was wrong.** Many accounts frequently mention his adulterous affair with 
Marilyn Monroe as a flaw. I don't think like that, although Marilyn's personality probably 
was not entirely not putrid, from what I've read about her. Bear with me now because I'm 
discussing other people, not myself. John F Kennedy was raised a Roman Catholic and had 
grand aspirations for uniting the country. He was a leading figure of the American Democratic 
Party. He wanted national unity but developed an animosity towards certain organizations such 
as the Central Intelligence Agency. In the grand scheme, the United States is tricky because 
it is extensive by landmass, with activity very much geared for commerce. John F Kennedy 
probably thought of himself as a nationalist. But he couldn't have been a strong federalist. 
His approach was similar to the Catholic Church's aspiration, during the medieval times, to 
accommodate for all good-doers of Western Europe. So John F Kennedy must have gone up against 
some American institutions during his political career. His risk-taking was egalitarian, in 
the conventional sense of socialism around the world. An agent sent to deconstruct the vicegrips 
supporting discrimination and prejudice throughout the needy land of the United States. His life 
is frequently cast in terms of tragedy, a good man albeit misguided? Incapable, perhaps? This is 
convincing to a person if John F Kennedy was supposed to be the one taking sole responsibility. 
Factually, the man lived in a rather tumultuous time period of political activity occurring in 
the United States. 

**I believe most of the Christian clergy is benign.** Accommodations from and involving any clergy 
may not be, either through repression (right-wing) or over-expansion (left-wing). In purist terms, 
although practice has long past purity, this religious dichotomy runs inverse to the political dichotomy 
(repression as left-wing through statism and over-expression as right-wing through commerce). Yes, it 
is a flawed division of understanding complexities and multipolarities. But rationality rarely steps 
outside of division by two before division by more. Some Christians believe that schism is a 
God-given right. Others see the Church as the umbrella, where rules to obey, by regulation and 
morality, can become more convoluted and unobvious through social rank. 

**Corporatist-Statist support can promote the wrong people. I believe it still can.** How about 
that, "corporatist-statist"? In the second article, I called the American Republican Party 
the Socialist-Capitalist Party because that's just how I feel about how it's been doing 
things. Laughably, the American Republican Party has done a much finer job of implementing 
the principle of "capitalism for the poor, socialism for the rich" than the American Democratic 
Party. Since the Obama years, the years when my mind matured enough to become politically 
observant, it is just my opinion that the Democrats have tried to accommodate for 
historically marginalized groups, groups that might have been imperiled without stronger 
government backing, on foundations of operating losses. It is not as though American Democrats 
were giving really excessive handouts to any person willing to just take the initiative to 
do something, express their true selves, and/or aim for higher than most. Democratic policies 
were a haphazard mixture of apologism, statism, and anchorage dragging down the upper halves. 
And American Democratic policies really did keep big money concentrated in the same hands. 
Therefore, I think Democratic policies did a better job of lying about modern contemporaries 
to the United Fruit Company and the East India Tea Company than the Republicans have been. They 
attempted to integrate them into quasi-statist governance superceding the federal state, instead of 
allowing for gambling spirits and proto-feudalism to become the thing, the mark of rulers and 
champions alike. I'm not going to take any more steps in advancing this claim, after reviewing 
the fact that some decisions are taken from circumstantial stress. Everyone, leaders and subjects, 
bends to stress, but not in the same way. The question is, is political affiliation a reliable variable 
in predicting how circumstantial stress affects decision-making? 

**The death of Western liberalism means the death of perspective wanting to keep matters open-ended.** 
Here is a multiple choice question. Would that be prejudice, a defense mechanism, ignorance, a combination

matters have already been at least half-made up? That's the question, not just for me.
